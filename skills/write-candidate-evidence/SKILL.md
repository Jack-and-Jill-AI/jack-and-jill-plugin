# Write candidate evidence

Use this skill when deciding who belongs on a shortlist or writing the evidence inside a candidate report.

A shortlist is usually 5 to 15 people you would defend in conversation. Rank and filter scores help order attention, but the recommendation must come from the candidate's profile and the assignment.

## Write evidence that supports a decision

For each candidate, name:

- the work they personally owned;
- the relevant scale, systems, customers, or outcomes;
- the trajectory or domain evidence that matters here;
- the strongest unresolved question.

Use specific profile evidence. Do not infer work authorization, intent, relocation willingness, attendance preference, or job scope when the profile does not state it.

## Present stretch candidates honestly

A strong candidate who misses one stated preference may still deserve a separate "Worth a conversation" tier. State the mismatch first, then explain why the evidence may justify a conversation.

The rule is simple: caveat, never conceal. Advocacy does not justify hiding a mismatch from the principal or overselling the role to the candidate.

## Keep recommendation separate from authorization

Committing a candidate and recommending them in a report does not authorize outreach. Wait for explicit principal direction. If that direction arrives outside Jack & Jill, record it with `record_feedback`. Use decision `intro` only when the principal explicitly wants an introduction.

Nothing in search, ranking, or shortlist publication authorizes `send_message`, `request_intro`, or `send_intro_email`.
